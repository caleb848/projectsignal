Methodology

How health is scored

A transparent 0–100 score. Every project starts at 100 and loses points for six observable signals. There is no weighting model and no AI, just rules a PM can check and argue with.

Inputs and deductions

Each input is capped so no single signal dominates. Maximum total deduction: 100.

  • Timeline riskContingency used −6 · QA window compressed −12 · QA at minimum −20 · launch date moves −25max −25
  • Budget variance−2 per 1% the forecast exceeds the approved budgetmax −20
  • Open high-impact risksCritical risk −10 · High −5 · Medium −2 (open or monitoring)max −20
  • Delayed approvals & dependencies−5 per waiting item older than 3 business days or past its deadlinemax −15
  • Resource constraints−5 per team over 100% this week or next where the project has 8+ hours bookedmax −10
  • Milestone delays−5 per open milestone past its planned end datemax −10

Bands

  • Healthy 90–100

    No material issues. Routine monitoring.

  • Watch 75–89

    Early warning signs. Review at weekly status.

  • At Risk 50–74

    Needs PM intervention this week.

  • Critical 0–49

    Escalate. Launch, budget or scope is in jeopardy.

Why these inputs

The score favours leading indicators (buffer, waiting items, capacity) over lagging ones (percent complete).

Timeline risk is measured by buffer, not by date. A delay that is absorbed by contingency is information; a delay that compresses QA is a risk; a delay that moves the launch is an escalation. The dependency engine works out which of those is happening.

Waiting items count before they become late milestones. An approval sitting with a client for four days is the earliest reliable sign of a slip. It costs points as soon as it crosses three business days.

The score sits beside PM status, not instead of it. A PM can mark a project On Track while the score says Watch, as with Horizon. That gap is a prompt for a conversation, not an override.
